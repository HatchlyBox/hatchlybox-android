summary: lead the eye with contrast, framing and depth; give every screen one focal point

## Method
- **One focal point per screen**: the most contrast, saturation, detail and light go there; everything
  else recedes. `visual check` "detail by thirds" shows where detail sits; "no focal point" warns when
  it's spread evenly.
- **Depth in 3–5 layers**: far (sky), mid (mountains/buildings), near (playfield), front (foliage, vignette).
  Far layers: less contrast, smaller, slower parallax.
- **Silhouettes**: characters and key objects must read as a solid shape alone. Test: fill with black.
- **Scale contrast**: grandeur comes from tiny next to huge (a small figure before an enormous castle).
- **Framing**: vignette, overhanging branches or architecture at the edges pull the eye inward.
- **Lines and diagonals**: roads, rivers and light shafts lead to the goal; diagonals add energy.
- **Negative space** around the focal point and the UI.
- **Phones**: portrait has a tall, narrow frame. Put the action in the middle third, HUD at the top under
  the safe area, thumbs at the bottom. In landscape keep the centre clear for action and the thumbs at
  the sides.

## Validation
Sheet: where does the eye go first? It should be the goal. `visual check`: the largest cell in "by thirds"
should be where the focal point is; warnings "very busy" or "no focal point" mean simplify the background.
