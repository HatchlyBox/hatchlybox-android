# Privacy policy

Hatchlybox for Android and Hatchlybox for computers (Linux, macOS, Windows). Last changed 26 October 2026.

## The short version

- Hatchlybox has no account, no servers of its own and no analytics. Nothing is sent to us.
- Your creations, settings and keys stay on your devices.
- What leaves your device goes where you send it: to the AI helper you chose while it works for you, to your own
  devices when you sync, and to GitHub or Google Play only when you connect them.

## What stays on your device

- Your requests, your creations (apps, games, widgets, websites) and their history.
- Your settings, your home screen and its widgets.
- The keys Hatchlybox makes or you give it: app signing keys, GitHub and Google Play sign-ins. Play keys can be
  locked with a password.
- Logs and crash reports. A crash report leaves the phone only if you tap Share and choose where it goes.

## What leaves your device, and only when

### The AI helper you choose

Hatchlybox makes things with an AI helper: Claude Code (Anthropic), Codex (OpenAI) or OpenCode (with the model
provider you set up in it). You sign in to it with your own account. While it works on a creation, it sends that
helper's company your request, the creation's files and the output of the tools it runs. That company handles them
under its own terms and privacy policy, with your account. Hatchlybox runs each helper in a sandbox on computers so
it only sees the creation it works on (phones can't sandbox it the same way).

### Downloads

Setting up and updating downloads software from each project's own servers: Alpine Linux, Node.js and npm packages,
the AI helpers, Java, Gradle and Maven libraries, Google's Android SDK, Godot and its export templates, and, if you
ask for it, Google's FunctionGemma model from Hugging Face. Those servers see your device's internet address and what
it downloads, as with any download.

### Sync between your devices

Sync goes straight from one of your devices to another on your network. Pairing uses a code or six digits you
confirm on both screens, and everything sent is encrypted end to end with keys only your paired devices have. If you
set up a relay yourself, it passes encrypted bytes it can't read.

### What you connect

- GitHub: when you connect it, Hatchlybox creates and updates repositories for the creations you choose, with your
  account.
- Google Play: when you connect a Play developer account, Hatchlybox uploads the releases you prepare, with the
  store listing and pictures you approve.
- Phone actions: when you ask for one (a message, a call, an event), Hatchlybox does it with the app or permission
  it needs, after showing you what it will do. The accessibility service reads the screen only while an action you
  asked for runs.

## Permissions on Android

Hatchlybox asks for each permission when a feature first needs it: notifications (setup and job progress),
installing and uninstalling apps (the ones you make), contacts, calendar, calls and SMS (phone actions you ask
for), and the accessibility service (actions in other apps). You can say no; the rest keeps working.

## Children

Hatchlybox isn't made for children under 13 and doesn't knowingly collect anything from anyone.

## Changes and questions

When this policy changes, the date above changes, and the app shows its welcome screen again if what you agreed to
changed. Questions: open an issue on Hatchlybox's GitHub page.
